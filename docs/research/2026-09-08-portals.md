## 0. The regulatory fact that drives everything

Before comparing portals: since REGA's real-estate marketing & advertising rules, **a FAL licence alone does not let you advertise a property in Saudi Arabia.** Every individual advertisement needs its own **advertising licence (ترخيص إعلان عقاري)**, tied to the title deed and to a registered brokerage contract with the owner.

Aqar's own help centre states this bluntly (**VERIFIED**, https://help.aqar.fm/ar/articles/8015333, article dated 16 January 2025):

> «رخصة فال ما تكفي لنشر إعلانك، يلزمك ترخيص لكل إعلان تنشره من الهيئة العامة للعقار»
> *"A FAL licence is not enough to publish your ad — you need a licence for every ad you publish, from the General Authority for Real Estate."*

**The broker's path to a compliant ad (VERIFIED, same source):**

1. Hold a valid FAL licence for brokerage and marketing (Bona has this).
2. **Register a brokerage contract (عقد وساطة) with the property owner** on REGA e-services.
3. Issue an **advertising licence** covering the ad data, and pay REGA's ad-licence fee.

The brokerage-contract step is the gate, and it is worth understanding because it is the real operational cost of listing anywhere. On REGA e-services (**VERIFIED**, same source) you:

- choose your role — **«وسيط فرد» (individual broker) or «وسيط منشأة» (establishment broker)**;
- open «عقود الوساطة» → «إنشاء عقد جديد»;
- enter the **electronic title-deed number (رقم وثيقة الملكية الإلكترونية / الصك)** and the **owner's ID number** (national ID / commercial registration / unified 700 number);
- REGA **verifies the deed data automatically**;
- enter the owner's mobile, contract start/end dates;
- select the **«التسويق» (marketing) scope — this is what enables the advertising licence to be issued**;
- enter your marketing fee, accept terms, confirm by OTP;
- the contract is **sent to the owner for approval**.

### Why this is good news for Bona

That role selector — **«وسيط فرد»** — is the single most important finding in this report. **REGA's own contract flow has a first-class path for a natural-person broker with no Commercial Registration.** Bona's individual FAL 1100313556 is sufficient to register brokerage contracts and therefore to issue advertising licences. You do not need a سجل تجاري to be legally advertising.

The constraint is commercial, not legal: portals sell their *bigger* packages to establishments, and a CR unlocks multi-user seats, logo branding and the unified 700 number. See the per-portal sections.

> **Deep regulatory detail** — the exact ad-licence fee, validity period, penalty schedule, platform obligations, and whether **bona-real-estate.com itself** counts as an advertising channel requiring the licence number, are covered in §7. That last point is not optional trivia: if your own site is an advertising channel, every listing on it needs a licence number too.

## 1. Aqar / عقار (sa.aqar.fm) — **strong value, held for review (see §8)**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **الأفراد البرونزية** — *Individuals Bronze* | **1,999 SAR/year** | 120 | 55% | 1 | Search + marketing requests; listing priority boost |
| **المنشآت الفضية** — *Establishments Silver* (marked "الأكثر شيوعاً" / most popular) | **5,999 SAR/year** | 400 | 60% | up to 10 | + logo watermark on listing images, unified 700 number displayed, higher support priority |
| **المنشآت الذهبية** — *Establishments Gold* | **15,999 SAR/year** | 1,200 | 65% | up to 20 | + dedicated account manager, top support priority |
| **المنشآت البلاتينية** — *Establishments Platinum* | **36,999 SAR/year** | 3,000 | 80% | up to 50 | as Gold, larger |
| Custom | NOT PUBLISHED | — | — | — | «تحتاج باقة أكبر؟» → contact sales |

All tiers are **valid for one year**.

**Independently corroborated.** Aqar's help centre article «الأسئلة المتكررة بخصوص الاشتراكات» (**VERIFIED**, https://help.aqar.fm/ar/articles/8761341, updated **21 June 2026**) repeats the same four tiers and slot/user counts, and states:

> «تم إيقاف الباقات الشهرية حاليًا. بإمكانك الاشتراك في الباقات السنوية **بدءً من 1999 ريال**.»
> *"Monthly packages are currently discontinued. You can subscribe to annual packages **starting from 1,999 SAR**."*

Two further points from that article, both **VERIFIED** and both commercially significant:

- **Annual only.** Monthly billing has been withdrawn — there is no low-commitment trial. 1,999 SAR is the minimum entry ticket.
- **No success fee.** «لا توجد رسوم بعد البيع أو الإيجار على المشتركين» — *"there are no post-sale or post-rental fees on subscribers."* Aqar takes **no commission on closed deals**. For a luxury broker whose single transaction fee can dwarf the subscription, this makes the effective cost of Aqar close to a rounding error.
- Downgrades are only possible at renewal (consistent with the packages page).

**Mechanics — VERIFIED from the FAQ on the same page:**

- **What is a "slot" (مساحة)?** «المساحة تتيح لك نشر إعلان واحد مرخص لمدة 30 يوم أو حتى إغلاق الإعلان أو انتهاء الترخيص (أيهم أقرب)» — one licensed ad for **30 days**, or until the ad is closed or the licence expires, whichever comes first. So 120 slots ≠ 120 simultaneous listings for a year; it is 120 × 30-day listing-months. A permanently-live listing consumes ~12 slots/year.
- **Extra slots are dynamically priced** by district supply/demand and category — high-demand districts cost more per slot. Subscribers get the stated discount off the non-subscriber rate. **Actual extra-slot SAR price: NOT PUBLISHED** (varies per district).
- **Upgrades** allowed any time with a pro-rata discount; **downgrades are not** — you can only change tier at renewal.

**Paid promotion (optional upsell).** Aqar runs a self-serve boost product, «خدمة تمييز الإعلانات» (**VERIFIED**, https://help.aqar.fm/ar/articles/8783029, dated 7 April 2026): you pick a listing, set a **daily budget** and campaign end date, and Aqar lifts its ranking in search results and within the neighbourhood/city, showing an estimated impression forecast for your budget. **There is no fixed rate card — the cost is whatever daily budget you set.** Useful for pushing one hero listing; not a required spend.

### The killer feature: Aqar issues your REGA ad licences **free**

**VERIFIED**, banner and FAQ at https://sa.aqar.fm/payment-gateway/packages, 2026-09-08:

> «للوسطاء المرخصين المشتركين: لفترة محدودة يمكنك إصدار التراخيص من خلال عقار مجانًا بعد إصدار عقد الوساطة»
> «عند نشر إعلانك من خلال عقار، نقوم بإصدار ترخيص الإعلان من الهيئة العامة للعقار نيابةً عنك مجانًا (يتطلب وجود عقد وساطة مع المالك). هذه الميزة متاحة لجميع المشتركين ولا تحتاج لدفع رسوم الترخيص بشكل منفصل.»

*Translation:* for subscribed licensed brokers, **for a limited period**, Aqar issues the REGA advertising licence **on your behalf, free**, once you have a brokerage contract with the owner — you do not pay the licence fee separately. Available to all subscribers.

This is a material saving and, more importantly, removes the per-listing REGA admin from your workflow. Note the two conditions: **you must be a paying subscriber**, and **you must already have the owner's brokerage contract**. Aqar also says brokerage-contract issuance will move into Aqar itself "soon" (ستتمكن من إصدار عقد الوساطة من خلال عقار قريبًا) — currently you still do that step on REGA e-services.

**Caveat:** "لفترة محدودة" (limited period) — this is a promotion, not a permanent term. Do not build a cost model that assumes it lasts forever.

### Individual FAL vs company CR

**VERIFIED — an individual FAL holder is explicitly catered for.** The entry tier is literally named **«الأفراد البرونزية» (Individuals Bronze)** at 1,999 SAR/yr. The three tiers above it are named **«المنشآت»** (establishments). The individual tier is capped at **one user** and does **not** include logo watermarking or the unified 700 number — both of which are establishment features.

So: Bona can open a fully compliant, paid Aqar broker account on FAL 1100313556 with **no CR**, today, for 1,999 SAR. The CR only becomes worth getting when you want multiple agents, brand watermarking on images, or a 700 number.

### Feed / bulk upload / API

**NOT PUBLISHED — and I checked the obvious place.** Aqar's «شركاء النجاح» page (https://sa.aqar.fm/partners) turns out to be a **commercial partners logo wall** — Riyad Bank, Bank AlBilad, SAB, STC, Al Saedan Real Estate, the Ministry of Municipal & Rural Affairs, the General Authority for Endowments, Munaseb financing — **not** a technical integration programme. **VERIFIED: no API, no data feed, no CRM integration and no developer documentation is mentioned anywhere on it.**

The only integration route is the custom-package sales contact («تحتاج باقة أكبر؟ تواصل معنا»). **Treat Aqar as manual/app-based entry** for planning purposes, and ask sales directly whether a feed exists for Gold/Platinum accounts.

*Practical implication for Bona:* at 11–40 listings you do not need a feed. Manual entry is fine and gives better control over photography and copy, which matters more in luxury than it does in volume.

### Audience fit for luxury

Aqar is **mass-market by volume but is also where the actual Jeddah supply lives**, including the top end.

**VERIFIED evidence (2026-09-08):**

- **Jeddah villas for sale on Aqar: ~2,681** (North Jeddah 2,525 + South Jeddah 156), read off https://sa.aqar.fm/فلل-للبيع/جدة. Compare Bayut 1,600+ and Property Finder **171**.
- The first page of Jeddah villa results included listings at **7,500,000 SAR** (Al Basateen), **2,970,000 SAR** (Durrat Al Arous), and a 3,000,000 SAR Obhur Al Janoubiyah villa 400 m from the beach with a detailed luxury spec (Italian lift, York central AC, full smart-home). Genuine upper-mid and luxury stock is present and transacting.
- App Store listing claims **2M+ monthly active users, SAR 233 billion of listings, 400M+ searches/month** (https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/.../id879992882 — *self-reported by the developer; treat as directional, not audited*).

Aqar is not a luxury-*positioned* brand — you will sit next to 579,000 SAR apartments. But Saudi buyers at every price point start on Aqar. For a Jeddah brand, absence from Aqar is absence from the market.

### Verdict: **HOLD FOR THE 3-MONTH REVIEW — 1,999 SAR/yr**

> Superseded by §8. On raw value Aqar is the best buy in this document; it lost on segment, because a new
> luxury brand should not spend its first year positioned beside mass-market stock. The numbers below stand.

Cheapest verified entry of any portal, explicitly built for an individual FAL holder, the deepest Jeddah supply and demand, and it removes your REGA ad-licence admin for free. There is no argument for skipping it.

**Important correction to those headline numbers.** Bayut's own help centre says credit consumption is **dynamic**, not flat (**VERIFIED**, https://help.bayut.sa/hc/en-us/articles/16336930939538, dated 12 January 2024):

### Individual FAL vs company CR — **individual is accepted**

**VERIFIED**, https://help.bayut.sa/hc/en-us/articles/16335512685714 ("What are the guidelines for posting ads on Bayut.sa?"):

> "You must be an active subscriber and have an account on Bayut.sa. You must have a **FAL license** to be able to publish your listing … Also provide a **commercial registration number or national identity number. (This depends on the number you used when you issued your FAL license)**. Provide a **valid advertising license number** to publish your ad on Bayut."

That parenthetical is decisive: Bayut accepts **whichever identifier your FAL was issued against**. An individual FAL issued against a national ID is explicitly supported. **No CR required.**

### REGA advertising-licence enforcement — **hard-blocked**

**VERIFIED**, same article: *"To publish your listing on our website, it requires a valid ad license."* The listing form has an advertising-licence-number field and posting is gated on it; Bayut links out to a "Learn more about the ad license" resource pointing at REGA. Bayut also runs a whole **"REGA Compliances"** help section (https://help.bayut.sa/hc/en-us) and markets itself as "REGA Certified … among the first firms licensed by the Real Estate General Authority."

Bayut's blog also confirms the three-part requirement — valid FAL, **registered brokerage agreement with the owner**, and a valid advertising licence — and describes issuing the ad licence via the real-estate-advertisements tab, specifying the brokerage agreement, entering property data and photos (https://www.bayut.sa/blog/en/invest-in-saudi/rega-regulations/).

**Bayut publishes the licence data, and it is the best compliance UI of any portal I looked at.** On a Jeddah villa detail page (**VERIFIED**, https://www.bayut.sa/en/property/details-87995132.html, loaded 2026-09-08) there is a dedicated **"REGA Verified Information"** panel containing:

- FAL License Number
- **Advertising License Number**
- **License Issue Date** and **License Expiry Date**
- Ad Responsible Info
- The licensed advertising channels: *"Licensed platform, Bulletin board, Social media platforms, Radio, Other"*
- *"The location is fetched and verified by REGA"*

Two things worth extracting from that panel, because they shape Bona's whole operating rhythm:

1. **Ad licences expire.** There is an explicit expiry date. Listings must be re-licensed, not set-and-forget. This is exactly why Aqar's slot model bills in 30-day units.
2. **The licence enumerates the channels it covers — and "Social media platforms" is one of them.** That is direct evidence that the advertising licence regime extends beyond portals to Instagram/Snapchat/X posts, and that the channel set is declared when the licence is issued. Bona's social marketing is in scope. (See §7.)

*Minor note:* the licence number appears on **detail pages only**, not on search-result cards — so you can audit a specific competitor listing, but not scan the market at a glance.

**Profolio™** is Bayut's listing-management platform, bundled with a paid package (**VERIFIED**, https://help.bayut.sa/hc/en-us/articles/18342196693138 — "you must first purchase a package, which grants access to Profolio"). Profolio covers leads management, listing optimisation, agency operations and insights, plus a credit consumption log.

**Public XML/CSV feed spec: NOT PUBLISHED.** Bayut/EMPG do operate CRM integrations in the group's other markets, so a feed almost certainly exists for larger accounts — but it is **gated to paid tiers and not publicly documented**. Ask during the quote call.

### Audience fit for luxury

**Better positioned than Aqar for an English-speaking / expatriate / investor luxury audience**, and materially weaker on raw Saudi supply.

**VERIFIED evidence (2026-09-08):**

- **Jeddah villas for sale: 1,600+** (https://www.bayut.sa/en/for-sale/villas/jeddah/) — about 60% of Aqar's Jeddah villa count.
- Bayut surfaces exactly the districts Bona targets: an **Al Shati** "luxury villa" feature listing, multiple **Obhur Al Shamaliyah** 5–6 bedroom villas, **Al Rawdah** (17 villas), and a **Durrat Al Arous direct-sea-view villa at 4,800,000 SAR**. Observed price range on that page ≈ **1,150,000 – 6,000,000 SAR**.

### Verdict: **BUY — Bronze, expected SAR 6,990/yr, confirmed in writing on the call**

Bayut is the right *second* portal: it reaches the English-language and investor luxury audience Aqar under-serves, and its verification badges help a new brand borrow credibility. But you cannot evaluate it without a number, and the credits model means a luxury Jeddah listing burns ~2.4× the headline credit cost. **Call 920035076, get the Bronze/Silver quote in writing, and compare it against what the same money does on Aqar plus paid social.**

## 3. Property Finder KSA (propertyfinder.sa) — **skip for now**

### Cost 2026

**NOT PUBLISHED.** Property Finder KSA sells subscriptions denominated in **Credits / Listing Tokens**, set in an Order Form at contract signature. No public rate card, no published credit price.

**Enquiry route:** https://www.propertyfinder.sa/company-registration/en/ ("Are you a registered real estate broker or developer? → Register now"). Support: support@propertyfinder.sa.

**VERIFIED contract terms** (https://www.propertyfinder.com/sa/terms-and-conditions/) — and these are unusually aggressive, so read them before signing anything:

- Credits are set at the start of the **Credit Term**; **unused credits expire and do not roll over**. Top-up credits expire too.
- **"No right to terminate this Contract before the end of the Contract Term under any circumstances."**
- **No downgrades** during the term. No pausing, no cancelling, **no refunds** on credits.
- Property Finder may **unilaterally change how many credits a listing costs** during your contract term ("at our sole discretion").
- They may demand a **security cheque for the full contract value** within 7 days of signing; access is limited after 5 days if you don't provide it.
- Non-payment: access limited after **3 days**, account suspended and **all listings removed** after **7 days**, terminated after **60**, and the **entire unpaid balance becomes immediately due**.

For a brand-new boutique with limited budget, a non-cancellable annual contract backed by a blank-cheque security instrument is a serious risk. This is the single strongest argument against Property Finder in year 1.

### Individual FAL vs company CR

**Not explicitly resolved in the SA terms.** The T&C require that you are the *"holder of a valid license as a real estate broker, hotel apartment or real estate developer in the Location"*, that you *"obtain and possess necessary permits to market and publish the Listings"*, and that you can prove authority to act for owners (including power of attorney). It says nothing about a Commercial Registration or VAT certificate, and explicitly disclaims responsibility for your permits.

**UNVERIFIED / practical read:** the registration flow is branded *company* registration and PF's KSA sales motion is agency-oriented, so expect them to ask for a CR in practice even though the contract doesn't require one. **Confirm before investing time.**

### REGA advertising-licence enforcement

**Property Finder holds its own REGA licence** (https://static-assets.propertyfinder.com/documents/REGA-LICENSE.pdf — **VERIFIED** the document link exists on propertyfinder.sa).

**VERIFIED (indirect):** Property Finder's integration documentation states that **for KSA listings, fields have special handling due to REGA integration** — i.e. the ad-licence number is a first-class field in their KSA data model, enforced at the API/feed layer.

**Observed:** the licence number was **not displayed** on the public listing cards I loaded — and unlike Bayut, I found no equivalent "REGA Verified Information" panel. PF's KSA compliance presentation is weaker than Bayut's.

### Feed / bulk upload / API — **best in class, and the only real one here**

This is Property Finder's genuine differentiator:

- **XML feed integration** — connect a publicly accessible XML feed URL produced by your CRM to your PF account; create users and publish/update/edit listings from a third-party CRM into **PF Expert**. Built to a published Property Finder XML feed specification (`<property>`, `<price>`, `<location>`; mandatory fields include property ID, type, price). Docs: https://support.propertyfinder.ae/hc/en-us/articles/22147369096978 *(the article is indexed and its content is summarised in search results; the page itself 404'd/403'd when I tried to load it directly on 2026-09-08 — **content UNVERIFIED by direct read**)*.
- **Enterprise API** — JSON, standard HTTP codes, covers their product suite. Docs at https://api-docs.propertyfinder.net/enterprise-api/index.html — **password-protected**, so gated to contracted partners. **VERIFIED that it is gated** (I hit the password wall).
- Integration contact: integration.support@propertyfinder.ae
- Third-party CRMs (e.g. Propertybase) ship Middle East portal integrations targeting PF.

If Bona ever runs a large managed portfolio out of its own system, PF is the portal that can ingest it automatically. That day is not year 1.

### Audience fit for luxury

**Strong brand, near-absent inventory in Saudi.** This is the finding that decides it.

**VERIFIED (2026-09-08, https://www.propertyfinder.sa/en/search):**

- **Villas for sale across ALL of Saudi Arabia: 1,199.**
- Of which **Jeddah: 171.** (Riyadh 366, Al Khobar 125, Dammam 119, Taif 111, Al Ahsa 42, Makkah 36.)

**171 Jeddah villas versus Aqar's ~2,681 and Bayut's 1,600+.** Property Finder is the dominant luxury portal in the UAE; in Saudi it is a distant third at roughly **6% of Aqar's Jeddah villa depth**. A Bona listing there would be well-presented and largely unseen. Buyer-side liquidity, not seller-side polish, is what a new brand needs.

### Verdict: **SKIP in year 1. Revisit in year 2 if inventory exceeds ~50 live listings.**

The contract terms are the harshest of any portal, the price is unknown, and the Saudi audience is the thinnest of the three majors. The XML/API story is excellent but solves a problem Bona does not yet have. Reconsider when (a) you have a CRM worth feeding, and (b) PF's Jeddah depth has grown.

## 5. Haraj (haraj.com.sa) — **free, do it, buy nothing**

Haraj is Saudi Arabia's mass-market classifieds board — enormous reach, no luxury positioning, and a
presentation layer that cannot show a home well.

- **Cost: free to post.** Haraj sells paid boosts/promotion.
- **Account: a Saudi mobile number.** No CR needed.
- **Ad licence: still required.** Being free does not exempt a real-estate ad from the REGA advertising
  licence — Art. 3 of the bylaw covers all channels (see §7).
- **Luxury fit: poor.** Buyers at Bona's price point do not shortlist from Haraj. But sellers and
  intermediaries read it, and land in particular does move there.

### Verdict: **POST FREE — buy no boosts.**

Post the land plots and anything at the lower end of the portfolio. Treat it as a free net, not a channel to
invest in. Every riyal of promotion budget belongs somewhere else.

## 7. The REGA advertising-licence regime — the operating reality

This section is the compliance backbone. Everything above assumes Bona gets this right.

### 7.1 The governing instrument

**VERIFIED.** The controlling text is **اللائحة التنظيمية للتسويق والإعلانات العقارية** (Regulatory Bylaw on Real Estate Marketing and Advertising), issued by the REGA Board under the Real Estate Brokerage Law (Royal Decree **M/130, 30/11/1443H**).

| Item | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| PDF cover date | شوال 1447هـ / April 2026 | VERIFIED (official PDF) |
| Umm Al-Qura publication | **14/11/1447H = 1 May 2026** | VERIFIED |
| Entry into force | **On publication → 1 May 2026** (Art. 12) | VERIFIED |
| **Grace period** | **NONE** — Art. 12 grants no transition window | VERIFIED (by absence across all 12 articles) |
| Articles | 12 | VERIFIED |

**Correction to a common misreading:** per-advertisement licensing is **not new in 2026**. REGA launched the ad-licence service on **2 February 2023**, replacing the older "رقم معلن" (advertiser number). The May-2026 bylaw *codifies, broadens and hardens* it, and **repeals** the two 1442H board resolutions that previously governed the area (Art. 11) — so any guidance still citing «ضوابط الإعلانات العقارية» (resolution 3/14/م/21) is dead law.

**The regime has been fully in force since 1 May 2026. No grace period ran, and none is coming.**

### 7.2 Per advertisement, not per broker — confirmed twice

**VERIFIED**, from two independent primary sources:

1. **Executive Regulation of the Brokerage Law, Article 6:** *"The real estate broker and real estate service provider shall obtain a Real Estate Advertisement License **for each advertisement** after paying the applicable fees."*
2. **2026 bylaw, Art. 4 (and the SPA release):** an independent ad licence is required **before publishing any** real-estate advertisement.

The FAL licence is the **practice** licence. The ad licence is the **per-property publication** licence. **If Bona has 11 listings live, Bona needs 11 ad licences.**

### 7.3 Where you issue it, and what it costs

**VERIFIED.** The service runs on REGA's electronic brokerage platform. Note the hostname — it is **not** `fal.rega.gov.sa`:

| Purpose | URL |
|---|---|
| **Issue an ad licence** (individual broker) | `https://eservicesredp.rega.gov.sa/main/IndividualBroker/Elanat` |
| **Verify/inquire a licence** (public, free) | `https://eservicesredp.rega.gov.sa/auth/queries/Elanat` |
| Platform login | `https://eservicesredp.rega.gov.sa/auth/login` |
| Service description (AR) | https://rega.gov.sa/rega-services/eservices/إصدار-ترخيص-إعلان-عقاري/ |
| National Platform service card | https://my.gov.sa/ar/services/20735 |

**Fee: SAR 50 per advertisement — VERIFIED**, from the Saudi National Platform's own service catalogue (my.gov.sa service 20735): «تكلفة الخدمة: 50» and «مدة الخدمة: فوري» (instant). The **inquiry** service (20774) is free.

*Conflicting figures, and how to read them:* a **SAR 299** figure appears on Aqar's blog — that is almost certainly **Aqar's retail price to property owners**, not the government fee. A **SAR 150** figure on a private agency site is explicitly *that agency's* service fee. REGA's own page unhelpfully says only «متغير بحسب نوع الخدمة». **Budget SAR 50/listing and confirm in the payment basket on first issuance.**

### 7.4 Prerequisites and the deed-verification chain

**VERIFIED — Bylaw Art. 4(1)** requires: advertiser data and **capacity (صفة)** with supporting documents; the **deed registration number (رقم صك تسجيل ملكية العقار)**; the rights being advertised; and an effective contact method.

**VERIFIED — REGA's service page** states the only prerequisite is **«عقد وساطة يتواجد فيه نطاق تسويق»** — a registered brokerage contract whose scope includes marketing — and that required document uploads are **«لا يوجد» (none)**, because everything is pulled from government registries.

The chain:

1. Broker creates a **brokerage contract** on REGA e-services: electronic deed number + owner's ID (National ID / CR / unified 700) + owner's mobile + term + **marketing scope**.
2. **REGA auto-verifies the deed** against the Real Estate Registry. This is the anti-fraud gate, and why no uploads are needed.
3. **The owner approves the contract via Nafath.**
4. Only then can the broker issue ad licences against it — selecting the ownership document, ad purpose, **advertising channels**, and price.

**Issuance itself is 12 steps and instant** once the contract exists.

> **Operational consequence:** the bottleneck is never the SAR 50 or the portal. It is **getting the owner to approve a brokerage contract via Nafath**. Every listing Bona wants to advertise legally requires that owner action first. Build the client conversation around it.

### 7.5 Expiry — and why this becomes a recurring chore

- **VERIFIED — Art. 5(1)(ز):** the ad must display the **licence expiry date**. An expiry always exists.
- **VERIFIED — Art. 6(7):** the licensee must remove the ad **«فور انتهاء الغرض منه، أو انتهاء تاريخ صلاحية ترخيصه، أيهما أسبق»** — immediately when the purpose ends **or** the licence expires, **whichever is earlier**. **A sold property must come down at once**, not at expiry.
- **VERIFIED — Art. 8(6):** the portal must independently remove the ad the moment the licence lapses.
- **VERIFIED — Brokerage Law Art. 7(2):** every brokerage contract must have a defined term; where none is agreed, the default is **90 days**. An ad licence cannot outlive its contract.

**UNVERIFIED but consistent:** the licence runs with the brokerage-contract term, and Aqar states owner listings are licensed for a maximum of one year.

> **Recommendation:** negotiate **12-month** brokerage contracts, not the 90-day default. Otherwise every listing needs re-licensing quarterly.

### 7.6 Penalties — the real schedule, and a wrong number in circulation

**VERIFIED — Brokerage Law Art. 19(1):** warning; suspension up to 1 year; cancellation; and/or a fine **not exceeding SAR 200,000**. Art. 19(2): **fines double on repeat within three years** (ceiling SAR 400,000). Art. 19(4): a cancelled licensee waits **3 years** to reapply.

**VERIFIED — the actual tariff** (Executive Regulation, Appendix 1 penalty schedule, Class A / 1st / 2nd / 3rd occurrence):

| Violation | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practising **without a valid licence** | SR 5,000 | 10,000 | 20,000 |
| Practising **after licence expiry** | SR 1,000 | 2,000 | 4,000 |
| False information to obtain a licence | SR 10,000 | 20,000 | 40,000 |
| **Omitting the licensee's name or licence number from an advertisement** | **Warning** | **SR 1,000** | **SR 2,000** |
| Brokering without holding ownership documents | Warning | SR 2,000 | 4,000 |

**Read this correctly.** The widely-quoted **SAR 200,000** is the **statutory ceiling**, not a first-offence tariff — REGA's spokesperson quoted it to the press in April 2026 and it has been recycled ever since. Real first-offence exposure for a **missing licence number is a warning**. That is not a licence to be sloppy, but it does mean the correct response to this report is *methodical compliance*, not panic.

**A SAR 1,000,000 platform fine figure circulating in aggregator summaries is UNVERIFIED and almost certainly wrong** — no such number exists in the Law (capped at 200k) or the schedule.

**Gap worth flagging to counsel:** the 1444H penalty schedule predates codified per-ad licensing and has **no line item for "publishing an ad without an ad licence."** Enforcement currently routes through the unlicensed-practice or missing-number items. Expect REGA to refresh the schedule.

### 7.7 **bona-real-estate.com is itself an advertising channel — and is currently non-compliant**

**This is the most consequential finding in the whole report, and the easiest to miss.**

**VERIFIED, from the regulation text:**

- **Art. 3** — scope covers **«جميع ممارسات التسويق والإعلانات العقارية»** (*all* marketing and advertising practices), "and in particular" six enumerated channels: social platforms, broadcast/print, exhibitions, **electronic real-estate platforms**, billboards, and any other means the Board designates. The operative word is *جميع* — the list is illustrative, not exhaustive.
- **Art. 5(1)** — the mandatory disclosure list applies to every ad **«ما عدا الإعلانات المنشورة في المنصات العقارية الإلكترونية»** — *except* ads on electronic real-estate platforms, which pull their data through REGA's API instead (Art. 5(3)).
- **Art. 1** — a منصة عقارية إلكترونية is a **licensed** app/website **that lets [third-party] advertisers publish** ads: a marketplace like Aqar or Bayut.

**bona-real-estate.com publishes Bona's own listings. It is not a licensed third-party marketplace, so it does not fall inside the Art. 5(1) carve-out.** It is an advertising channel under Art. 3, and **Art. 5(1) applies in full.** Every listing page must carry all eight items:

| # | Required on every Bona listing page |
|---|---|
| أ | The real or personal rights being advertised |
| ب | Property description, condition, data and location — **including anything affecting value or the buyer's decision** |
| ج | Attached services and rights — **including any existing disputes** |
| د | Contact details **identical to those on the licence application** |
| هـ | Advertiser's name |
| **و** | **The ad licence number, displayed prominently («إبراز رقم ترخيص الإعلان»)** |
| **ز** | **The ad licence expiry date** |
| **ح** | **The practice (FAL) licence number — 1100313556** |

**Current state — VERIFIED 2026-09-08:** https://bona-real-estate.com displays *"REGA FAL licence 1100313556"* in the footer and shows **~11 listings (5 villas/houses, 3 apartments, 3 land plots)**. It shows **no per-listing advertising licence number and no expiry date**. As built, the site does not satisfy Art. 5(1).

**The design-friendly fix — VERIFIED, Art. 5(2):** ad content may be reduced to **the licence number plus a QR code** resolving to the full disclosure set. For a luxury brand that does not want regulatory boilerplate crowding the page, a discreet licence line plus a QR is compliant and elegant.

**Two hard corollaries for the site build:**

- **Contact details must match the licence application exactly.** Art. 7(3) *prohibits* publishing contact details different from those submitted. The WhatsApp number on the site (+966593296933) must be the number on every ad licence.
- **Automate expiry.** Art. 6(7) requires removal the instant a licence lapses or a property sells. This belongs in a scheduled job keyed on licence expiry dates, not a manual checklist.

### 7.8 Social media is in scope too

**VERIFIED, three times over:** the bylaw lists «منصات ووسائل التواصل الاجتماعي» as the **first** enumerated channel (Art. 3(1)); REGA's FAQ defines the ad licence as covering visual, audio, written and electronic ads including **«في وسائل التواصل الاجتماعي»**; and the SPA release says the same.

Independently corroborated from the portal side: the **"REGA Verified Information"** panel on Bayut listing detail pages enumerates the licensed channels as *"Licensed platform, Bulletin board, **Social media platforms**, Radio, Other"* — the channel set is declared when the licence is issued.

**So:** every Instagram / Snapchat / X post advertising a specific Bona property needs a valid ad licence, must carry the number + expiry + FAL number (or the QR), and the channel must have been declared at issuance. Instagram is **not** a REGA-integrated منصة عقارية إلكترونية, so the Art. 5(1) carve-out does not rescue it.

**One extra licence to check — UNVERIFIED:** one industry source claims real-estate content on social media may *also* require a **موثوق (Mawthooq)** licence from the General Authority for Media Regulation, on top of the REGA ad licence. I could not verify this against a primary GMedia source, and it may only bite paid/influencer advertising. **Check before running any influencer campaign.**

### 7.9 Individual FAL vs establishment CR — the definitive answer

**VERIFIED, from REGA's own service pages:**

| | **رخصة فال — شخص طبيعي (individual)** | **رخصة فال — منشأة (establishment)** |
|---|---|---|
| Identity requirement | **Valid national ID — the only document** | **Valid CR including activity code 682010** |
| Qualification | 3 courses: الوساطة العقارية + التسويق العقاري + التسويق العقاري الإلكتروني | Same, taken by the المدير المسؤول |
| **Annual fee** | **SAR 300** | **SAR 1,000** |
| **Commercial Registration** | **NOT required** | **Required** |

Reinforced by the Executive Regulation: **Art. 3 (natural persons) lists no CR condition**; Art. 4 (legal entities) opens with *"Have a valid commercial registration…"*. Licence validity 1 year, extendable to 5 (Art. 7); renew within 60 days of expiry (Art. 8).

**Can a natural-person FAL holder advertise, broker and sign brokerage agreements without a CR? — YES. VERIFIED.**

REGA's own FAQ: *«وفق نظام الوساطة العقارية يتاح للأفراد ممارسة الأنشطة والخدمات التالية: الوساطة العقارية، التسويق العقاري، **الإعلان العقاري**، الاستشارات والتحليلات العقارية.»*

- **Broker:** yes — Law Art. 4 conditions practice only on holding a REGA licence.
- **Sign brokerage contracts:** yes — Exec. Reg. Art. 16 sets contract content with no CR condition, and the platform has a «وسيط فرد» role.
- **Issue ad licences:** yes — the issuance endpoint is literally `/main/IndividualBroker/Elanat`.

REGA restricts **facility management, property management and real-estate auctions to licensed establishments only** (Exec. Reg. Art. 2(4)). **Brokerage and marketing are not on that restricted list** — which is precisely why the individual track exists. There is **no REGA rule compelling a physical office**; that requirement follows from choosing the CR route, not from REGA.

> **⚠️ One thing to check, Abdulaziz.** An Aqar blog post (**UNVERIFIED**) claims a numbering convention where FAL brokerage licences begin with **6** and advertising licences with **7** (individuals 71…, establishments 72…). **1100313556 fits neither pattern.** Meanwhile the Jeddah listing I sampled on Aqar carried «رقم رخصة فال | 1200020594». Before relying on 1100313556 as a brokerage-and-marketing FAL, **log in to `eservicesredp.rega.gov.sa` and confirm which licence class it actually is, and that its scope includes التسويق (marketing)** — the ad-licence flow requires marketing scope. If the numbering convention is real, this is worth five minutes now rather than a blocked listing later.

### 7.10 Trading identity: freelance certificate vs CR, and VAT

**VERIFIED:** the FAL licence is regulatory permission to *practise*; it is **not** a commercial identity. To invoice, bank and contract as a business you need either **وثيقة العمل الحر** (MHRSD, via freelance.sa) or a **سجل تجاري**.

- **وثيقة العمل الحر** — Saudi nationals 18+; enables a commercial bank account tied to the certificate, receiving commissions, digital payment rails, and contracting with government and private entities. **Free to issue and renew — UNVERIFIED** (secondary source only; freelance.sa is a JS app that could not be read directly).
- **سجل تجاري** — **UNVERIFIED costs** from a secondary source: ~SAR 200/yr registry + from SAR 500/yr Chamber of Commerce + FAL at SAR 1,000/yr + premises; that source's first-year all-in estimate with a physical office is SAR 50,000–100,000.

**VAT — VERIFIED from ZATCA's own e-service page:**

- **Mandatory registration above SAR 375,000** in annual taxable supplies.
- **Voluntary registration above SAR 187,500.**
- **A CR is not a precondition** — ZATCA runs a dedicated **Individual VAT Sign-up** for "individuals engaged in economic activity in the Kingdom".

**Brokerage commission is a taxable service at 15%**, even though residential property sales themselves are VAT-exempt (they bear RETT instead). **The threshold test runs on commission income, not deal value** — so Bona is nowhere near SAR 375,000 of commission until it is closing meaningful volume. Registration is not an immediate concern, but track it.
